Earlier this year I completed fieldwork in Europe on energy communities and their ecosystem, from cooperatives like Ecopower and Som Energia to neighbourhood energy communities, one-stop shops and energy houses in Spain, Portugal, Belgium and Austria.

I am now at the point of presenting fresh findings from this research.

I propose a 30-minute talk presenting key insights from the field, combined with the institutional theory that helps make meaning of them, drawing on scholars from Adam Smith and Friedrich Hayek through to Elinor Ostrom. The heart of it is the idea of

Energy communities as local institutions that generate the knowledge, trust and shared norms that markets and governments alone cannot. Energy communities reliably produce agency: participation in delivering the future people are asked to want, rather than passively hosting it.

Second, trust behaves like accumulating capital, built by volunteers at folding tables and then borrowed by the wider system, increasing trust in reliable providers in markets, reassuring financiers to support energy communities with their work and lowering barriers for households to participate in energy efficiency actions.
