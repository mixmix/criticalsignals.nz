Join NZIA Gold Medal laureates Hugh Tennent & Ewan Brown for a lunchtime talk
exploring the two halves of material selections, and how poetic and
sustainability considerations have come together to form the firm's award
winning work.

Founders of the award winning architectural firm Tennent Brown Architects, Hugh
and Ewan have sustained a consistent values-based vision that has progressively
become more influential. Their work shows us the responsibilities we face as a
profession and illuminates pathways for others to follow. They've daylighted
ideas (and a waterway) that others have barely dared to contemplate. Despite its
onerous demands, they've embraced the Living Building Challenge (LBC), which
pushes architects to address the industry's significant contribution to the
climate crisis.

**Ghost Forests** is a photographic and video project that explores the
ecological histories hidden within New Zealand's architectural heritage. The
work considers the relationship between buildings like the Wellington's Old
Government Buildings—one of the world's largest surviving timber (Kauri)
buildings—and the forests that supplied its construction material. The project
proposes the idea of architecture as ecological heritage. Historic timber
buildings are often discussed through the lenses of architecture, craftsmanship
and preservation. Less frequently acknowledged are the forests that made them
possible. Ghost Forests asks visitors to consider buildings as repositories of
environmental memory.
The exhibition is open for 2 weeks - [see details here](/programme/andy-spain-ghost-forests-exhibition/).
