**Doors open 5.00pm for drinks, nibbles and kōrero. Workshop starts at 6.00pm.**

Critical Signals believes that communities have the right to reuse and collectively manage empty, underused and neglected spaces for mutual aid and solidarity activities.

That means community gardens instead of an empty parking lot, a tool library in that shed that hasn't been used for 3 years, or an abandoned building at risk of becoming a target for property speculation being transformed into a space providing local welfare services. This is what "Urban Commoning" looks like.

In the second part of our Urban Commoning series, we're hosting a workshop on rewriting the law to empower communities to use the spaces around us. Attendance at previous events is not necessary.

Giuseppe Micciarelli is flying in from Italy, where he is a political theorist and legal sociologist who was instrumental in paving the way for the recognition of grassroots urban commoning practices in Italian administrative regulations.

Last time, you brought a photo of an urban space, green area, or building that has been abandoned or fallen into disuse—perhaps one whose history you already know or would like to investigate further. This time, we will delve further into a few of the spaces we discovered last time.

We will explore the changes we can claim to make so that local communities can benefit from the spaces in their neighbourhoods. We will also look at the challenges and pitfalls of dialogue between communities and public institutions, focusing on the barriers to bringing neglected spaces back into use that arise from public and private property owners. Together, we will explore how communities can better understand these obstacles and find ways to overcome them.

There will be nibbles, discussion, and the chance to make a change in the city you live in.
