<article class="person">
  <h2 class="person__name">Sophie Jerram</h2>
  <div class="person__photo--empty" aria-hidden="true"></div>
  <p class="person__role">Collective space maker / Researcher / Hustler</p>
  <div class="person__body">
    <p>A believer in the power of creative collective action in real space and time. Developer of social practice through the Letting Space projects, a co-developer of Urban Dream Brokerage and a long term Trustee of the Vogelmorn Precinct — a mixed use community site in South Wellington. Mother of two epic 20-somethings.</p>
    <h4>What does Critical Signals mean for you?</h4>
    <p>A chance to build collective action; to bring people together for creative, joyful and somatic regulation in this moment of rapid change.</p>
    <h4>The most exciting work in your community?</h4>
    <p>Calling out the invisible biases and naming alternatives — Māoriland in Ōtaki; Papawhakaritorito Trust's work on soil sovereignty; WeALL's economic framework, Wesley Community Action and Co-shop — Wellington is rich with abundance.</p>
    <h4>Who's inspiring you right now?</h4>
    <p>People seeking each other to resist forces of capital and violence that appear 'inevitable'. Francesca Albanese; Carole Cadwalladr; Bisan from Gaza; Tamatha Paul — anyone who is bravely using a platform to continually offer alternative future pathways. All the community around Critical Signals who have been brought in to offer their wonderous creative offers!</p>
  </div>
</article>

<article class="person">
  <h2 class="person__name">Emily Malcolm</h2>
  <img class="person__photo" src="/images/design/people/emily.jpg" alt="Emily Malcolm">
  <p class="person__role">Third space enthusiast / Environmental Studies student / Victoria Rescue junior responder</p>
  <div class="person__body">
    <p>Nō Ingarani, Kotirana me Aerana tōku tūpuna
E noho ana au ki Te Whanganui-a-Tara
Nō te whānau Malcolm mātau
Ko Emily tōku ingoa</p>
    <p>Bringing knowledge of environmental justice, climate change and sustainability to this space, Emily is a final year Environmental Studies student at Te Herenga Waka – Victoria University of Wellington. Joining the university's response team, NZRT-7 Victoria Rescue, and attending awesome earth science field trips have deepened her enthusiasm for natural hazard response, recovery and preparedness. She has a particular interest in community resilience, and the power that building relationships with one another has in improving our shared world.</p>
    <h4>What does Critical Signals mean for you?</h4>
    <p>I had such rich experiences throughout last year's programme as an attendee, and I'm so grateful to bring my energy into the planning and running of things this year. Critical Signals is full of new opportunities, both to work with inspiring and ambitious people, and to contribute with my ideas and by recruiting new volunteers.</p>
    <h4>The most exciting work in your community?</h4>
    <p>Finding ways to live a great life without ridiculous costs; making tea and hosting events with my flatmates, running weekly "third space" hangouts with friends, regular visits to Bikespace, doing our best to remain optimistic in the face of newsfeeds / war crimes / politicians.</p>
    <h4>Who's inspiring you right now?</h4>
    <p>Persistence from people who want a better world. Those who look me in the eye and take flyers from my hand in the street, everyone who keeps learning.</p>
  </div>
</article>

<article class="person">
  <h2 class="person__name">Ash Holwell</h2>
  <img class="person__photo" src="/images/design/people/ash.jpg" alt="Ash Holwell">
  <p class="person__role">Design Activist</p>
  <div class="person__body">
    <p>I am a maker of spaces and platforms for people to connect, do their work, be themselves and build the worlds they want, together.</p>
    <h4>What does Critical Signals mean for you?</h4>
    <p>An incredible vehicle for drawing together diverse modes of practice and inquiry into actionable, community led projects that change both the immediate surroundings of Pōneke, and make this approach more possible for others.</p>
    <h4>The most exciting work in your community?</h4>
    <p>Food systems and land use changes; urban densification; active transport options; young people creating and driving culture changes; the appreciation of Te Matapihi te ao Nui as a public, collective, commonly owned resource and taonga — more of that!</p>
    <h4>Who's inspiring you right now?</h4>
    <p>The commitment of the people around Ngā Puna Wai Ora Newtown School in fighting adversity and actively building a better world for each other, together, every single day.</p>
  </div>
</article>
